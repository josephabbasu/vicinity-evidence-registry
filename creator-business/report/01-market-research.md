# Phase 1 — Market Research & Opportunity Analysis

## 1. Methodology

**Sources synthesized:** YouTube category/CPM data (vidIQ, OutlierKit, MilX, Lenostube, AIR Media-Tech), Google Trends patterns, Google/YouTube search-question datasets (Semrush, Glimpse, RankDots), the S&P Global FinLit Survey (GFLEC), Pew Research and NerdWallet/Ramsey personal-finance studies, creator-economy niche reports (Teachable, Mediacube, Lemonlight), plus consumer-behavior literature on habit formation and repeat viewing. Where a claim rests on a single commercial source or on model knowledge rather than a verifiable dataset, it is flagged with a confidence level.

**Confidence key:** 🟢 High (multiple independent sources) · 🟡 Medium (directional, single-source or estimated) · 🔴 Low (assumption, stated explicitly).

**Process:**
1. Enumerate humanity's biggest *recurring* problems (not news cycles).
2. Map each to observable search/viewing demand.
3. Generate 12 candidate channel businesses spanning those problems.
4. Score each on 10 weighted criteria; stress-test the winner against failure modes.

## 2. The world's biggest recurring problems (demand foundation)

Problems that billions of people re-encounter continuously, ranked by breadth × recurrence × willingness to seek help online:

| # | Recurring problem | Scale of need | Search behavior | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Managing money** (saving, debt, budgeting, investing, scams) | ~3.5B adults not financially literate (S&P FinLit); US adults answer only ~49% of basic finance questions correctly | Perennial top "how to" cluster; spikes every January, every payday, every crisis | 🟢 |
| 2 | **Health, sleep, stress** | Global; chronic conditions + sleep problems affect 30–50% of adults | Massive, but YMYL-sensitive and misinformation-crowded | 🟢 |
| 3 | **Earning & career** (jobs, interviews, skills, side income) | Every working adult, refreshed each career stage | High recurrence; strong overlap with #1 | 🟢 |
| 4 | **Learning English / languages** | ~1.5B English learners | Enormous, evergreen; competitive but under-supplied at quality tier | 🟢 |
| 5 | **Using technology / AI** | Every knowledge worker post-2023 | Explosive growth; tool-specific content decays fast | 🟢 |
| 6 | **Relationships & parenting** | Universal | High demand; hard to systematize, ad-sensitive | 🟡 |
| 7 | **Food & cooking fundamentals** | Universal daily need ("how long to boil eggs" ≈ 200k mo. searches) | Evergreen but low CPM, hyper-saturated | 🟢 |
| 8 | **Life admin** (taxes, insurance, contracts, bureaucracy) | Every adult, every year | High-intent, underserved, but jurisdiction-fragmented | 🟡 |
| 9 | **Housing** (renting, buying, repairs) | Universal; big-ticket decisions | High CPM adjacency to finance | 🟢 |
| 10 | **Studying & exams** | Global student population | Seasonal-recurrent, loyal cohorts, moderate CPM | 🟢 |

**Observation:** Problems #1, #3, #8 and #9 are facets of one meta-problem — *making good decisions about money* — which is simultaneously the highest-monetizing content category on YouTube. This convergence of maximal need and maximal monetization is rare.

## 3. Most-searched questions (selected, money cluster)

Recurring high-volume questions with year-round demand (Google/YouTube, global) 🟢:

- how to save money / how to make money / how to make money online
- how to budget (as a beginner / on a low income)
- how to get out of debt / pay off credit card debt
- how to invest / how to start investing with little money
- what is compound interest / what is inflation / why is everything so expensive
- how do credit scores work / how do taxes work / how do banks work
- is X a scam / how to spot a scam
- how much money do I need to retire / to feel secure
- salary negotiation / how to ask for a raise

These questions share three properties: they never stop being asked, every new 18-year-old asks them fresh, and the asker is in a *decision moment* — the highest-value attention advertisers can buy.

## 6. Underserved audiences (the gap analysis)

Rather than copying incumbent channels, we looked for demand that incumbents structurally cannot serve:

1. **The non-US money learner (THE gap).** Finance YouTube's biggest channels are US-centric: their advice (401(k), Roth IRA, FICO, US index funds) is irrelevant or misleading for ~90% of YouTube's 2.5B+ users. Local-language creators exist but are fragmented, often unvetted, and frequently monetize by pushing products. **No dominant channel teaches transferable money principles in global English with cited evidence.** 🟢 gap existence / 🟡 gap size
2. **The distrustful viewer.** Post-FTX, post-finfluencer-scandals, audiences actively search "is X legit". A channel whose brand *is* verification — citations on screen, corrections policy, no product-pushing — serves an unmet trust need. 🟡
3. **Low-income and first-generation earners.** Most finance content assumes investable surplus. Content for people managing scarcity (debt triage, informal income, remittances, inflation survival) is high-demand and nearly empty at the quality tier. 🟡
4. **New adults (16–24) globally.** Gen Z scores worst on financial literacy (~37% in 2024 P-Fin index) while facing the most financialized economy in history. Shorts-first acquisition fits this cohort perfectly. 🟢

## 8. High-engagement & low-competition observations

- **Engagement:** money content triggers comments (personal stories, country-specific questions) — a recommendation-system asset. "What's this called in YOUR country?" is a built-in comment engine. 🟡
- **Low-competition wedges within the niche:** scam anatomy/forensics; inflation explained for high-inflation countries; remittances & cross-border money; "your first salary" series; money psychology with actual citations. Each has high search volume and weak incumbent coverage. 🟡
